Study design
• Study design is the procedure under which a study is carried out
Study design is the procedure under which a study is
carried out.

Examples
• Descriptive Studies
• Descriptive studies aim to provide a snapshot of a situation or
phenomenon without analyzing cause-and-effect relationships.
They answer questions such as “what is happening?” or “how
often does it occur?” These studies often use surveys, case
reports, or observational methods.Example: A cross-sectional
survey assessing the awareness of radiation protection practices
among radiographers in Saudi Arabia.Example: A case report
describing a rare presentation of thyroid carcinoma detected on
ultrasound.

Analytical Studies (Observational)
• Analytical studies : go beyond description to explore associations and possible
relationships between variables. They are often used when experimental methods
are not feasible.
• Cross-sectional studies: Measure both exposure and outcome at the same point
in time.
• Example: A study examining the prevalence of musculoskeletal pain among
sonographers and its association with years of experience.
• Case-control studies: Compare patients with a specific condition (cases) to those
without (controls) to identify possible risk factors.
• Example: Investigating whether long working hours are associated with higher risk
of neck pain in radiographers.
• Cohort studies: Follow a group over time to assess how exposures affect
outcomes.
• Example: A prospective cohort study tracking radiation exposure levels in
interventional radiologists and the subsequent risk of cataract development.

Experimental (Interventional) Studies
• In experimental studies, the researcher actively manipulates an
intervention or exposure to observe its effect on an outcome. T
• Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs): Participants are
randomly assigned to intervention or control groups to test the
effect of a treatment.
Example: A trial comparing the diagnostic accuracy of a new MRI
sequence (3D SPACE FLAIR) with standard FLAIR imaging in
detecting multiple sclerosis lesions. hese studies provide
stronger evidence for causality.

Continue>>experimental studies
• Quasi-experimental studies: Similar to RCTs but without
randomization.
Example: Implementing a new radiology reporting template in
one hospital and comparing efficiency with another hospital
using the old system.

Systematic Reviews and MetaAnalysesThese

• Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses: These studies
synthesize results from multiple primary studies to provide a
high level of evidence. A systematic review identifies, appraises,
and summarizes findings, while a meta-analysis statistically
combines data from different studies.
• Example: A systematic review analyzing the effectiveness of
ultrasound elastography in differentiating benign and malignant
thyroid nodules.
• Example: A meta-analysis pooling data from several RCTs on the
accuracy of digital breast tomosynthesis compared to
conventional mammography.

Summary

•Descriptive → Describe “what is.”
•Analytical → Explore associations.
•Experimental → Test cause-and-effect.
•Systematic/Meta-analysis → Summarize existing evidence.
